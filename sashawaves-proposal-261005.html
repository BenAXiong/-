How the pieces fit together

The big question is what new learning products would be worth making and be viable. I’ve outlined three ideas in Phase 2 below. Since you just developed an exam-prep product, I thought we could focus the next phase on products that are fun, useful and also structured. Each combines content people might enjoy with guidance that helps them understand and use the language. They would need new content, recordings, or production work, so I’d like us to compare them before committing to that work.

As I've told you, I also have on my hands two tools that could interest you and learners. The first is a powerful extension to analyze Amis words on any web page and instantly display definitions, morphology and example sentences. The second is a player for the ILRDF videos with basic features. I'd love to see people get value from them. They're both nearly ready to publish so I believe this is a manageable first step: we can release something useful and see how we work together while discussing Phase 2.

FreeChrome desktopReady to test this week

Look up Amis while reading a webpage, explore meanings and examples, and keep useful material for later.

Free Small effort Skool

Give a newcomer a useful Amis win in 5–10 minutes after discovering 語浪/Skool, then offer one clear next step.

A compact experience for people arriving from Insta: a few short 2-3 min videos where Osay welcomes them personally and shares what she loves about the language. Listen & understand. Hear & respond. Why try it
Turn social-media curiosity into actual learning. The smallest of these three experiments can help us understand what newcomers find useful.
Main challenge

  • The next step: guide the user to their next destinations according to their needs.

  • Should the next step lead to a class, community, paid product, another learning experience?

Possible evolutions

This is mostly standalone and a stepping stone.

None planned.

Stop / pause

Mission Packs

Paid Medium effort Sashawaves.com

Prepare for real Amis situations with natural clips, careful teacher audio, and structured practice.

A pack contains short videos clips of native speakers saying day to day sentences (think Speak app). About 50 clips around a situation (buying 'icep, learning to fish, listening to a story, or simply introducing yourself). The sentences are authentic but simple, they comewith translations and concise usage help. Features to drill them (e.g. cloze, rapid-fire Q&A) helps the learner understand what they might hear and practice what they could say. Very valuable for speaking/listening, the core of many learners' goals. ,

Why try it
A low-commitment paid step between free tools and longer classes or membership. Audio and language assets can support future products. Straightforward scaling. Potentially an awesome way to help Amis people advertize their livelihood (think Sawtoy, restaurant owners, musicians, wood carvers, etc)
Main challenge
Find interested peakers and select natural language that is teachable, useful, and consistent enough for a focused pack.
One possible learner moment Before visiting someone in the buluo, a learner practices an introduction, hears how different speakers might answer, and rehearses a short exchange.
What a first pack could contain
  • One clear situation and outcome. 50 core sentences for a substantial pack as a working concept, adjustable after prototyping.
  • Teacher-verified language, selected native clips, slow recordings, translations and brief grammar/usage help.
  • 1-3 drill types, with quick natural answers and fuller practice variants where useful.
  • Easy access on phones.
  • Optional sharing on social media.
  • Small community challenges: points for speaking and recording others.
  • The core product does not depend on AI.
Questions to decide together
  • Which situation makes the clearest useful paid promise? Self-intro, buying groceries, chatting with Ama, ...?
  • How to avoid conflict with Rekad content? Providemore depth, breadth and tools. Rekad are a good complement.
  • How many natural clips are enough, and which drills offer the most value?
  • How to find reliable speakers willing to cooperate? People who need advertizing.
  • How to solve the dialect issue? Create packs for Southern/Coastal Amis first.
  • How would someone buy and access a pack through the website/Skool?
  • What price and production workload would make a first pack worthwhile?
Possible evolutions
  • Later packs could turn into skill & culture leanrning. Centered on a person, craft, place, or business.
  • Branching choices, role-play, register variants
  • Mission completion could become interaction upgrades within packs.
  • Add deeper review or bounded AI help where it serves the learning experience.
What would tell us to scale, revise, or stop?

Track purchase, starts/completion, drills/replay, return use, confidence/usefulness feedback, and interest in another pack. Compare revenue with content and engineering hours, ongoing support, and any conversion into classes/community.

Scale

People buy, use the pack, report useful practice, and want another.

Revise

Purchase interest and actual use diverge. Adjust the promise, content balance, drills, access, or price.

Stop / pause

Demand or learner value remains weak after a bounded revision, or each pack costs too much to sustain.

Amis Microdrama

Paid (subscription) Large effort Sashawaves.com

Amis Netflix. Follow humoristic short drama with learning help.

Modern situations, recurring characters, and natural Amis in 30–60 second episodes. The story brings people back. Subtitles, lookup, and replay help them understand and practice.

Why try it
An entertaining format that can travel through social media and showcase storytellers and performers. Unique content, great learning value, branding boost. The content can support other learning formats.
Main challenge
Good dialogue, performers, pacing, and production coordination. Writing and filming/editing may dominate the cost.
One possible learner moment A learner watches a one-minute buluo comedy scene, checks an unfamiliar line, learns a new expression, replays it, then opens the next episode to find out what happens. They rewatch the content are start using the lines in real life.
What a small pilot could contain
  • One storyline and a small recurring cast, 6–10 episodes of 30–60 seconds.
  • Vertical video suited to phones, natural language and reliable subtitles/translations.
  • Light production that gives the story and language a fair test.
  • Minimal learning support: lookup and replay, with shadowing or bookmarking.
  • A free sample followed by a paid season or subscription test.
Questions to decide together
  • Who is the audience, and which genre would they keep watching?
  • Who can write and perform dialogue that is natural and entertaining?
  • What is the smallest season that still tests continuation and anticipation?
  • Which language variety, learning tools, and free-to-paid boundary fit?
  • Who can film/edit, and what production workload is realistic?
Possible evolutions

Discussion around the plot, shadowing sahring or reenactment, and Mission Packs built around scenes could follow. Longer seasons and richer tools depending on what the small pilot teaches us.

What would tell us to scale, revise, or stop?

Watch episode completion, continuation from episode 1 through the season, repeat viewing, free-to-paid conversion, and “would you watch another season?” feedback. Compare production hours/cost per completed viewer and payer, plus platform maintenance.

Scale

Viewers keep watching, want another season, and some pay at a production cost we can repeat.

Revise

The first episode attracts interest but continuation drops. Rework the story, pacing, language support, or paid boundary.

Stop / pause

The small season does not sustain viewing or paid interest, or production demands outweigh the result.
